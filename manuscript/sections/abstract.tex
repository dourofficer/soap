\begin{abstract}
Failure attribution in large language model (LLM) multi-agent systems---pinpointing the decisive error step at which a failed trajectory became unrecoverable---is a prerequisite for debugging, yet it remains stubbornly hard, with the best methods locating the correct step in only a small fraction of cases. Existing approaches read the entire interaction log and emit a single verdict, either by prompting an LLM judge or by fine-tuning a model on annotated failure traces; the most accurate of them depend on costly labeled corpora or strong proprietary judges. In this paper, we take a different route and cast attribution as \emph{per-step anomaly scoring} over the internal representations of an off-the-shelf proxy model, requiring no labels, no fine-tuning, and no access to the system that produced the trajectory. We then identify a previously unnamed obstacle that hampers any contextual scorer: because a decisive error corrupts the context of all subsequent steps, anomaly accumulates downstream, and a naive predictor systematically selects a step \emph{after} the true error. To address this, we propose \crr{} (Causal Residual Rescoring), which subtracts from each step's score the portion of its anomaly inherited from causally upstream steps, weighted by the attention mass the proxy routes into each predecessor and modulated by a single discount strength. \crr{} is training-free, annotation-free, and can be plugged into any per-step base scorer. Extensive experiments across base scorers, proxy models, and both Who\&When subsets show that \crr{} consistently improves step-level attribution---by up to \PH\% over the underlying base scorer---while matching or surpassing training-based and advanced-prompting methods that rely on labeled data or proprietary judges. \TODO{Need revising}
\end{abstract}
