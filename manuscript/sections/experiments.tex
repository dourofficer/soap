\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.} We evaluate on three failure-attribution benchmarks comprising five evaluation subsets, each providing per-trajectory annotations of the decisive-error step and the responsible agent: \text{Who\&When}~\citep{whoandwhen}, with its \emph{algorithm-generated} (\text{WW-AG}, 126 trajectories) and \emph{hand-crafted} (\text{WW-HC}, 58) subsets; \text{CORRECT-Error}~\citep{correct} (\text{CE}, 2{,}226 trajectories); and \text{TraceElephant}~\citep{traceelephant}, with its Captain-Agent (\text{TE-Cap}, 85) and Magentic-One (\text{TE-Mag}, 91) subsets. Together they span short and long trajectories, QA- and code-centric domains, and two generating systems. \hl{Each subset is partitioned \emph{by trajectory} into train/validation/test splits of $30/20/50\%$.} Full dataset statistics and preprocessing details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:datasets}.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Following \citet{whoandwhen}, our headline metric is Step-Level Accuracy: the fraction of trajectories whose predicted decisive step exactly matches the annotation. Agent-Level Accuracy, ranked-set metrics (step@$k$, MRR), and tolerance variants ($\pm1,\pm2$) are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare against three families:
(i)~\emph{prompt-based methods} --- All-at-Once, Step-by-Step, Binary Search~\citep{whoandwhen}, the voting method ECHO~\citep{echo}, the schema-retrieval method CORRECT~\citep{correct}, the fault-reasoning judge RAFFLES~\citep{raffles}, and the causal-graph method CHIEF~\citep{chief};
(ii)~\emph{trained LLM attributors} --- AgenTracer~\citep{agentracer} and GraphTracer~\citep{graphtracer}, attributors fine-tuned with reinforcement learning, both reproduced;
(iii)~a \emph{representation-based method} --- OAT~\citep{oat}, a one-class tracer trained on successful trajectories, the same family as \soap.
Some baselines can additionally be given the ground-truth task answer; for each baseline we report the \emph{stronger} of the two settings, marking cells that use the answer with \gtmark{}, and provide the complete with-/without-GT breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:gt}. \soap never consults the task answer. CORRECT additionally consumes the gold error annotations of other trajectories through its retrieval protocol (footnoted in Table~\ref{tab:main}). A Random predictor, computed analytically from trajectory lengths, is included as a floor.
% TODO: ECHO is not yet reproduced — finish or cut the row before submission.

\paragraph{Implementation details.} \soap reads a proxy language model $\proxy$ that did not generate the trajectories, exposing hidden states and post-softmax attention. We use two backbones throughout, Qwen3.5-9B~\citep{qwen3} and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B~\citep{deepseekr1}. To ensure a fair comparison, all baselines are reproduced under the same two backbones rather than transcribed from their original papers. The SVD reference $R$ is fit on the train split. All results are reported as mean $\pm$ std over three random seeds, each seed drawing an independent train/validation/test partition.

Unlike the benchmark corpora, which contain failures only, the generated trajectories are used as-is, regardless of task success. \subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:exp:analysis}


\textcolor{blue}{we also need experiments: 1) comparing with different scoring functions, such as embedding norms, perplexity (other uncertainty score, 3-4), random subspace, projection to top / tail components and full spectrum, etc. 2) studying the effect of rescoring by removing it completely; 3) studying the effect of attention-guided rescoring by still having uniform rescoring but not attention, and unnormalized (no uniform weighting, directly adding scores of later steps together) rescoring. 4) comparing with a simple position-based baseline, the addition part in the scoring function can be minus (-) the index of the step divided by the total number of steps, with a weigh $\lambda$}



The remaining analyses ablate the individual components of \soap, all under the same protocol: starting from the validation-selected configuration of Table~\ref{tab:main} (Appendix~\ref{app:anchors}), we vary exactly one axis and hold every other hyperparameter fixed; the anchor's own value therefore appears as one point of every sweep and coincides with the main-table result. Unless stated otherwise, ablations use backbone Qwen3.5-9B on WW-AG and WW-HC; DeepSeek-8B mirrors are in Appendix~\ref{app:deepseek-ablations}.
